ML / AI Research Engineer
Tokenization & Fine-Tuning

EloAI is testing a hypothesis that changes how domain-specialized LLMs are built. We need a research engineer who can run the experiment — not just read about it.

Full-time Remote Equity-included Early hire

The Hypothesis

Current domain-specialized LLMs inherit BPE tokenization from general-purpose models — which means "myocardial infarction" arrives at the model as three or four sub-word fragments. The model then has to reconstruct a concept from character statistics that were never designed to encode it.

EloAI's Mixture of Libraries (MoL) architecture solves this at the source: replace BPE with a domain-specific dictionary where high-frequency domain terms and phrases are assigned atomic IDs before training begins. The routing decision is deterministic, made once at encode time, and fully auditable — the opposite of a learned MoE gating function.

The core claim: a MoL-fine-tuned model reaches domain-expert performance at 1/5 to 1/10 the training data of a BPE baseline. Your job is to prove or disprove it, rigorously.

What You'll Do

  • Replace Qwen3 3B's BPE tokenizer with the EloAI Base64 dictionary and rebuild the embedding matrix for the new vocabulary
  • Initialize embeddings from FAISS vectors derived from domain-specific sentence transformer models — so the model starts with a semantically pre-organized embedding space, not random noise
  • Design and execute the four-configuration fine-tuning experiment: BPE baseline, MoL only, FAISS init only, MoL + FAISS — at multiple training data scales (100M, 500M, 1B, 5B, 10B tokens)
  • Run benchmarks, report perplexity and loss curves honestly, and write up what the data actually says — including if the hypothesis is wrong
  • Work with the EPA projector (Evaluation–Potency–Activity semantic space) to validate that compressed token IDs encode meaningful affective and semantic dimensions
  • Contribute to the verbalizer architecture — the component that generates natural language output from compressed internal state

Who You Are

  • You've worked below the model API. You know what happens when you swap a tokenizer, what breaks, and why. You're not intimidated by rebuilding an embedding matrix from scratch.
  • You design experiments, not demos. You define controls, write down your hypothesis before you run the code, and report results that falsify as much as confirm.
  • You understand information theory well enough to reason about why shorter IDs for higher-frequency tokens is a meaningful training signal — not just a compression trick.
  • You're comfortable with negative results. If MoL doesn't beat BPE at 1/10 the data, that's important to know. The goal is truth, not a paper that confirms the thesis.
  • You find the intersection of compression, semantics, and LLM training genuinely interesting — not just as a job description but as a research question.

What This Isn't

This is not a role where you run existing fine-tuning scripts and report numbers. The pipeline doesn't fully exist yet. You'll be building the experiment infrastructure while running the experiment — which means the research and the engineering are the same job.

Compensation is competitive for an early-stage research role: base salary, meaningful equity, and co-authorship on any research output. The MoL thesis paper (eloai.dev/research) is the foundation; the experiment is the proof.
